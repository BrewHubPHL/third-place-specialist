# Chapter 7: Understanding Popular Music Heritage Through the Lens of Third Place

## Core Idea

Popular music heritage is not only archives and canon—it is a set of **practices** (preserving, touring, walking, reuniting) that assemble communities between home and work. Istvandity, Baker, Collins, Driessen, and Strong apply **Oldenburg criteria** to DIY archives, online memory platforms, city walking tours, and reunion concerts, showing third place as a flexible lens for physical, mobile, and virtual gathering. Heritage work becomes a third place when it fosters conversation, levelling, regular participation, and care—often sustaining retirees and dispersed fan communities.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Oldenburg criteria (1989, 1996–1997, 1999)** — neutral ground, leveller, conversation main activity, accessibility, regulars/characters, low profile, playful mood, home away from home; ten indicators used explicitly for community archives.
- **Virtual third place (Soukup 2006)** — localisation, accessibility, presence via shared symbolic space online.
- **Third space (Wright 2012)** — discourse and participation patterns, not geographic co-location; shifts analysis from place to networked interaction.
- **Community of practice (Baker)** — newcomers assimilated into archive/museum workflows and social networks.
- **Heritage as practice (Roberts & Cohen)** — wider social, cultural, economic processes beyond "the music itself."
- **Doing-it-together / activist archivism (Collins)** — collective online history-making.
- **Temporary third place (Driessen)** — reunion concerts as fixed-moment, obligation-free levelling spaces.

## Key Concepts

- **DIY popular music heritage** — volunteer-run archives, museums, halls of fame; translocal communities of interest.
- **Affective archiving** — atmospheres of laughter, banter, and serious preservation coexisting (Baker 2015).
- **Social therapy of heritage volunteering** — caring for volunteers' health, medication advice, hospital runs (Australian Jazz Museum).
- **Virtual vs physical third place tension** — Oldenburg (1999) dismisses atomized online life; Web 2.0 challenges this via Birmingham Music Archive and dance-band forums.
- **Mobile heritage** — Melbourne Music Walk links heritage discourse to live venue third places; timing (day vs night) changes engagement from museum-distance to embodied participation.
- **Heritage reunion tour** — The Big Reunion as escape from adult obligations; childhood memory reformation; ticket cost challenges accessibility criterion.

## Mental Models

- **Heritage continuum** — physical archive → virtual archive → walking tour → temporary concert; third-place traits vary by position on continuum.
- **Practice over artifact** — what people *do* together (book clubs, working bees, forum threads) matters more than stored objects.
- **Leisure-between-roles** — heritage sites occupy the gap where dedication and enthusiasm replace employment identity (retirees especially).
- **Place–space split** — geographically anchored BMA vs interest-bound Yahoo/Facebook groups; Wright's third **space** fits the latter.
- **Heritage enacted in present** — ACDC Lane at night: graffiti heritage + Cherry Bar punters = past celebrated through current third-place use.
- **Conversation as preservation infrastructure** — talk that "hampers timely completion" of archival tasks is functional, not wasteful (Ohlandt).

## Anti-patterns

- **Museum-only heritage** — removing music from messy, noisy original contexts (Leonard); walking tours partly remedy this.
- **Oldenburg purity tests** — rejecting online or ticketed experiences because they fail neighbourhood walkability or low-cost access.
- **Platform sustainability neglect** — Facebook group content at greater loss risk than Yahoo archives (Baker & Collins 2016). *Since publication, Yahoo Groups removed hosted content (2019–20) and shut down in Dec 2020 — the platform-loss risk applies to every hosted platform, Yahoo included.*
- **Daytime walk tourism** — Melbourne Music Walk by day reduces venues to façades; misses third-place entry.
- **Forced cultural planting** — Albertslund-style expectation that immigrants grow heritage crops when free seedlings homogenize choices (cross-chapter echo).
- **Treating heritage venues as silent galleries** — Slater & Koo found Tate/South Bank lack conversation; incomplete third places.

## Worked Example

Four case strands demonstrate the continuum:

1. **Community-run archives (Baker)** — Heart of Texas Museum unifies town beyond country-music fandom; Australian Jazz Museum book clubs become seminars; intergenerational "wow moments" (Icelandic teen meeting grandmother via database). Maps cleanly to all ten Oldenburg indicators including **gift of friendship** and care for neighbourhood.

2. **Online archives (Collins)** — Birmingham Music Archive: 2500+ contributors; Barbarella's thread spans three years of memory, grief (Wayne's death), and localness. Golden Age of British Dance Bands: 759 Yahoo + 2219 Facebook members; offline meetups blur virtual/physical.

3. **Melbourne Music Walk (Strong)** — 3.5 km CBD route mixing heritage lanes (Amphlett), working venues (Cherry Bar, Forum), and present-tense bars; Saturday night enables partial third-place entry; sold-out gigs limit access.

4. **The Big Reunion (Driessen)** — ITV2 reformations → Hammersmith Apollo → 14-arena tour; attendees escape "work, money, housework" for playful fandom; pricey arena concessions strain Oldenburg accessibility.

## Key Takeaways

- Apply **Oldenburg criteria** flexibly to heritage *practices*, not only cafés and barbershops.
- Community DIY archives align most closely with classic third place; virtual and mobile forms require **virtual third place** or **third space** framing.
- Heritage volunteering delivers community care, productive retirement, and intergenerational bridging—third-place social functions with preservation outcomes.
- Design heritage experiences for temporal access: night walks and open venues beat daytime façade tourism.
- Acknowledge accessibility caveats (internet, tickets, platform fragility) when claiming third-place status online or at arena scale.
