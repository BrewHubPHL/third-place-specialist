# Chapter 6: Eyes on the Street — Third Places and Perceived Neighbourhood Safety

## Core Idea

Third places contribute to neighbourhood safety, but they are one piece of a multidimensional puzzle—not a standalone crime remedy. Holden tests whether Oldenburg's third places and Jane Jacobs' **eyes on the street** improve both **perceived safety** and **actual safety** across eight Gold Coast case study neighbourhoods spanning gated communities, conventional suburbs, shopping streets, and intentional eco-villages. The answer is qualified: third places add natural surveillance, yet context—urban form, socio-economics, gate control, alcohol outlets, accessibility—often overrides their effect.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Oldenburg criteria** — neutral ground, levelled status, light conversation, accessibility, regulars welcoming newcomers, low profile, playful mood; cafés, parks, clubs as typologies.
- **Jane Jacobs — eyes on the street** — clear public/private demarcation; natural proprietors watching sidewalks; continuous pedestrian users; mixed ages, tenure, and land uses.
- **Oscar Newman — defensible space** — territorial clarity (private/semi-private/semi-public/public); natural surveillance; maintenance signals guardianship; **Community of Interest** (age/lifestyle grouping).
- **Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED)** — Jeffrey/Heal/Laycock lineage; design triggers surveillance so criminals self-select out.
- **Prospect–refuge theory (Appleton; Loewen et al.)** — light + open space + real refuge = safest perceived environments; buildings with non-dangerous people present 24/7 (potential third places).
- **Maslow / Lang hierarchy** — physiological and safety needs interwoven with affiliation, esteem, and aesthetic needs in urban design.
- **Granovetter — strength of weak ties** — neighbouring through third-place access paths.

## Key Concepts

- **Perception vs actual safety** — fear of crime often diverges from victimization risk (Garofalo; Delbose & Currie); physical maintenance and community cohesion remedy fear (Shepherdson).
- **Neighbourhood vs community** — neighbourhood as physical place, community as social ties (Lang; Burton et al.); lived mental maps vs statistical SA2 boundaries (Alidoust et al.).
- **Internalization vs externalization of third places** — coffee shops must face the street for patrons to provide eyes; journey-to-place also counts as surveillance.
- **Third party policing** — managers/landlords as crime-control guardians (Mazerolle & Ransley).
- **Crime displacement** — successful control in one area pushes crime elsewhere (gated communities especially).
- **Active street edges** — shops, cafés, services lower crime vs blank frontages (Beavon et al.; Shu).

## Mental Models

- **Complex web, not linear fix** — safety links physiological provision, fear, affiliation, and urban form simultaneously (Lang).
- **Third place as surveillance node** — externalized venues + pedestrian routes = layered eyes; internalized venues contribute less unless visually connected.
- **Modifier stack** — when judging a third place's safety impact, scan: function, location, technology, lighting, maintenance, land use, density, socio-economics, activity level.
- **Gated trade-off** — Halcyon Waters: gate + fence may matter more than third places for actual crime; social privilege with possible displacement cost (Blakely & Snyder).
- **Intentional community multiplier** — Eco Village: low density but strong **Community of Interest** compensates via vigilant neighbouring (Newman 1981).
- **Shopping-street paradox** — most third places (Chirn Park, Mermaid Waters) yet among highest crime; alcohol outlets and accessibility amplify risk (Wo 2016).

## Anti-patterns

- **Blanket policy** — mandating third places everywhere to improve safety without site-specific analysis.
- **Assuming third places alone reduce crime** — conventional suburbs with high CPTED scores but few third places show weak actual-safety gains from eyes alone.
- **Ignoring third-place type** — bars and liquor stores correlate with violent crime; libraries and cafés with reduction (Wo; Peterson et al.).
- **Suburban Jacobs mismatch** — eyes on the street originated in mid/high-density mixed-use contexts; applying uncritically to cul-de-sac suburbia.
- **CCTV as false comfort** — "secured" space may increase fear while upgrading safety (Van Melik et al.).
- **Statistical neighbourhood boundaries** — policy from census units without lived-experience mapping mis-targets interventions.

## Worked Example

Eight Gold Coast sites (400 m diameter crime-map circles) scored 1–5 on eyes on the street, accessibility, pedestrian environment, lighting, and activity; third-place counts and offences per 100 persons compared:

| Group | Sites | Insight |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Hope Island | Halcyon (gated) vs Cova | Halcyon: 0.4 offences/100 vs Cova 2.5; gate control dominates |
| Chirn Park | Residential vs shopping street | Highest crime in residential (fewest third places); shopping street slightly lower despite many cafés |
| Mermaid Waters | Residential vs shopping street | Tavern/alcohol proximity elevates shopping-street crime |
| Currumbin Valley | Eco Village vs rural residential | Eco Village 1.28 vs rural 2.57; intentional community spirit + third places |

~65% of recorded crimes were property offences; day/night split nearly equal—lighting matters continuously. **More Coffee, Less Crime?** (Papachristos et al.) cited as supporting literature for café diffusion effects.

## Key Takeaways

- Third places improve **perception** of safety more reliably than **actual** crime rates in conventional neighbourhoods.
- **Eyes on the street** (Jacobs/Newman/CPTED) outweigh third places alone—well-lit, maintained pedestrian environments are the stronger lever.
- Match third-place interventions to context: function, externalization toward street, socio-economics, and alcohol land uses.
- Gated and intentional communities achieve safety through access control and social contract, with third places as supporting—not primary—mechanisms.
- Study each place before prescribing third places as a safety remedy; modifier interactions dominate simple correlations.
