# Glossary — Rethinking Third Places

**Bonding social capital** — Strong ties among similar people; trust and reciprocity within closed groups (Putnam; Ch 3, 8)

**Bridging social capital** — Weak ties across diverse groups; links to new information and integration (Granovetter; Putnam; Ch 3, 8)

**Broadacre City** — Frank Lloyd Wright's car-centred organic city merging farm, recreation, and local government (Ch 1)

**Characters / place champions** — Regular patrons who anchor third-place social life (Oldenburg; Ch 1, 8)

**Child-friendly city** — UNICEF/CABE/Play England framework for rights, participation, and playable public realm (Ch 4). The CABE and Play England documents are historical guidance; CABE merged into the Design Council in 2011.

**Club vs third place spectrum** — Locked, fee-based, obligation-heavy gardens vs open walkable porous sites (Ch 8)

**Community as planning rationality** — Ideals of cohesion deployed to discipline behaviour through built form (Ch 1)

**CPTED** — Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design; defensible space, surveillance, territoriality (Ch 6)

**Docile subjects** — Homogeneous, obedient residents imagined by Garden City and suburban models (Jacobs; Ch 1)

**Eco-Village** — Barton model balancing privacy, community, and city access (Ch 1)

**Eight Oldenburg characteristics** — Neutral ground, leveller, easy access, regulars, low profile, playful, home away from home, conversation primary (Ch 1–11)

**Emplacement** — Embodied, situated experience of place; feminist critique of abstract "neutral" space (Ch 2)

**Eyes on the street** — Jacobs: natural surveillance from mixed use and active frontages (Ch 6)

**First / second / third place** — Home, work/school, informal public gathering (Oldenburg; Ch 1)

**Garden City** — Howard's self-contained city-in-country with membership through participation (Ch 1)

**Gemeinschaft** — Face-to-face village community ideal invoked by planners (Ch 1)

**Granovetter weak ties** — Acquaintance links enabling information flow and neighbourhood connection (Ch 3)

**Layering** — Digital information overlaid on physical place; AR as potential third-place extension (Ch 9)

**Leveller** — Third place that reduces status distinctions among patrons (Oldenburg; Ch 1)

**Mehta & Bosson street qualities** — Imageability, enclosure, human scale, transparency, complexity (Ch 11)

**Neutral ground** — Space neither owned by home nor work where people meet voluntarily (Oldenburg; Ch 1)

**New Urbanism** — Calthorpe/Duany walkable mixed-use neighbourhood model; community as commodity risk (Ch 1)

**Over-the-fence conversation** — Casual bridging talk at garden boundaries (Ch 8)

**Place-making / green / lively planning** — Cilliers' three evidence-based urban development approaches (Ch 5)

**SIDE model** — Social Identity and Deindividuation Effects; online disinhibition dynamics (Ch 9)

**Social capital** — Networks plus norms of reciprocity and trust (Putnam; Ch 3, 8)

**Third place of mobility** — Transit environments as informal gathering during movement (O'Hare; Ch 10)

**Three zones of activity** — Zone 1 (pavement dining), Zone 2 (sidewalk), Zone 3 (street) for street life (Ch 11)

**Triumph of the space user** — Oldenburg: patrons repurpose establishments beyond designer intent (Ch 1)

**User modification** — Ability of patrons to adapt space; prerequisite for authentic third places (Ch 1)

**Virtual third place / third space** — Online or hybrid gathering resembling Oldenburg criteria (Ch 7, 9)

**Village Idyll** — Unwin/Reade Arts and Crafts suburban imagery propagated through planning (Ch 1)

**Weak ties** — Acquaintance-level links; social health for ageing populations (Ch 3)
