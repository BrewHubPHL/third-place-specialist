# Third-Place Planning Examples (Abstract)

Abstract scenarios showing how to apply patterns from this skill to neighbourhood planning. No live project names, policy IDs, or secrets.

## Scenario A — New master-planned neighbourhood centre

**Brief claim:** "Walkable town centre with community plaza and café strip will build social cohesion."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Run [planning-community-traps.md](../patterns/planning-community-traps.md) — map price point, car dependence, gate hours, resident-only amenities.
2. Run [oldenburg-eight-test.md](../patterns/oldenburg-eight-test.md) on proposed café, plaza, and any "community room."
3. Check [feminist-third-place-audit.md](../patterns/feminist-third-place-audit.md) for night-time use, visibility vs escape, childcare access.
4. Flag if Ch 1 anti-patterns apply: form checklist passed but conversation not primary.

**Likely outcome:** Recommend porous edges (non-residents welcome without purchase), library co-location, distributed seating, and programming budget for regulars—not a single branded "community hub."

## Scenario B — Age-friendly retrofit in car-dependent suburb

**Brief claim:** "On-site seniors lounge in MPC satisfies age-friendly policy."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Apply [ageing-social-health.md](../patterns/ageing-social-health.md) — map weak-tie destinations within 10-minute walk/transit when driving stops.
2. Cross-check [transit-third-place.md](../patterns/transit-third-place.md) if bus stop is the only external link.
3. Compare bonding (club lounge) vs bridging (library, main street café, open garden).

**Likely outcome:** Advocate shuttle-to-main-street third places, footpath continuity, and mixed-age seating at existing libraries—not internal amenity alone.

## Scenario C — Community garden allocation

**Brief claim:** "Secure fenced garden reduces vandalism; membership fee covers insurance."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Walk [community-garden-design.md](../patterns/community-garden-design.md) decision tree from [cheatsheet.md](../references/cheatsheet.md).
2. If locked: document as **club model** (bonding OK, bridging limited)—do not claim neighbourhood integrator.
3. If open: require walkable catchment, over-the-fence design, commons tasks, character regulars.

**Likely outcome:** Hybrid open hours + low fence; host orientation sessions at library third place (Ch 8 pattern).

## Scenario D — Main street revitalisation grant

**Brief claim:** "Street beautification and new planters will activate the strip."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Map Three Zones per [street-life-activation.md](../patterns/street-life-activation.md).
2. Prioritise pavement dining permits, soft edges, personalised shopfronts before beautification-only spend.
3. Reference Geelong observation methodology: count group activities by zone and time of day.

**Likely outcome:** Zone 1 outdoor dining policy first; hard façade remediation second.

## Scenario E — Smart city AR heritage trail

**Brief claim:** "AR layer turns downtown into a third place for tourists and locals."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Apply [digital-layers.md](../patterns/digital-layers.md) — conversation primary online or on sidewalk?
2. Audit platform governance: who can post, moderate, exclude?
3. Pair with physical gathering nodes (café, archive open day per Ch 7).

**Likely outcome:** AR annotates existing third places; scheduled in-person archive sessions anchor weak ties—app alone insufficient.

---

## Scenario F — Transit station upgrade

**Brief claim:** "Faster throughput and reduced staffing improve commuter experience."

**Agent workflow:**

1. Apply [transit-third-place.md](../patterns/transit-third-place.md) — is wait time designed as social gift?
2. Check connection to neighbourhood third places at trip ends (Mehta & Bosson qualities at precinct).
3. Flag anti-pattern: seats removed, dwell discouraged, advertising wraps replace civic space.

**Likely outcome:** Retain seating clusters, shelter, local retail frontage; measure social activity not just headway minutes.
