# Anti-Patterns

Consolidated mistakes agents must flag when reviewing neighbourhood plans, public realm briefs, or "community" development claims. Each item maps to a corrective pattern or chapter where one exists.

---

## Planning & community rhetoric

### ❌ Assuming mixed-use form creates third places

Master-planned "town centres" with retail pods and a labelled plaza pass New Urbanist checklists but fail Oldenburg when conversation is not primary, access requires purchase, or gates exclude non-residents.

**Why:** Built form ≠ informal social life (Bosman & Dolley, Ch 1).  
**Fix:** [patterns/planning-community-traps.md](patterns/planning-community-traps.md) + [patterns/oldenburg-eight-test.md](patterns/oldenburg-eight-test.md)

### ❌ Marketing "community" without mapping exclusion

Eco-villages, gated MPCs, and price-filtered developments claim gemeinschaft while excluding difference through car dependence, participation rules, or homogeneous "docile" resident profiles.

**Why:** Community as planning rationality naturalises who belongs (Ch 1).  
**Fix:** [patterns/planning-community-traps.md](patterns/planning-community-traps.md)

### ❌ Planner-controlled public space that resists user modification

Fixed furniture, no-tolerance policies, and aesthetic policing prevent Oldenburg's "triumph of the space user."

**Why:** Third places emerge from appropriation, not designer intent alone (Ch 1, 11).  
**Fix:** Allow informal adaptation; audit for user modification.

## Inclusion & safety

### ❌ Treating third places as gender-neutral by default

Oldenburg's "leveller" claim ignores patriarchal public–private divides, male gaze, and who performs invisible labour sustaining "convivial" spaces.

**Why:** Feminist geography shows neutrality is performed, not given (Ch 2).  
**Fix:** [patterns/feminist-third-place-audit.md](patterns/feminist-third-place-audit.md)

### ❌ Safety through surveillance without emplacement

CCTV, hostile architecture, and blanket visibility without escape routes, diverse programming, or bodily-scale transparency can exclude the very users safety claims to protect.

**Why:** Transparency and autonomy must balance (Ch 2).  
**Fix:** [patterns/feminist-third-place-audit.md](patterns/feminist-third-place-audit.md)

### ❌ Austerity closure of public amenities marketed as "activation"

Replacing staffed libraries, pools, and youth centres with pop-up events shifts labour to volunteers and removes durable third places.

**Why:** Leisure infrastructure is gendered and classed (Ch 2).  
**Fix:** Protect permanent neutral ground; don't substitute one-off programming.

## Ageing, mobility & social health

### ❌ Car-dependent suburban "community" for ageing in place

On-site clubhouse amenities cannot replace city-wide weak ties when driving cessation ends participation.

**Why:** Accessibility beats mobility; weak ties need reachable third places (Ch 3).  
**Fix:** [patterns/ageing-social-health.md](patterns/ageing-social-health.md)

### ❌ Assuming MPC internal amenities substitute neighbourhood sociability

Gated commons build bonding among similar residents but starve bridging ties across diverse neighbours.

**Why:** Granovetter weak ties require porous, walkable access paths (Ch 3, 8).  
**Fix:** Connect to external libraries, clubs, transit-linked centres.

## Design & CPTED

### ❌ CPTED visibility as sole safety intervention

More third places on a retail strip does not guarantee lower recorded crime—Chirn Park and Mermaid Waters counterexamples on the Gold Coast.

**Why:** Crime correlates with land use, density, socio-economics, alcohol outlets—not third-place count alone (Ch 6).  
**Fix:** [patterns/eyes-on-street-safety.md](patterns/eyes-on-street-safety.md)

### ❌ Prioritising physical equipment over social programming

Play structures, benches, and beautification without programming, regulars, or conversation primacy produce empty "public" space.

**Why:** Cilliers ranks social activity quality above generic CPTED visibility (Ch 5).  
**Fix:** [patterns/evidence-based-design.md](patterns/evidence-based-design.md)

### ❌ Hard-edge façades on main streets

Blank walls, deep setbacks, and corporate polish suppress Zone 2 sidewalk life even when Zone 1 dining exists nearby.

**Why:** Soft edges and personalisation activate street life (Ch 11).  
**Fix:** [patterns/street-life-activation.md](patterns/street-life-activation.md)

## Gardens, transit & digital

### ❌ Locked community garden as neighbourhood integrator

Fee-based, key-access, city-wide-interest clubs build bonding capital but fail bridging and Oldenburg neutral ground.

**Why:** Open, walkable, over-the-fence sites score higher on 8-test audit (Ch 8).  
**Fix:** [patterns/community-garden-design.md](patterns/community-garden-design.md)

### ❌ Transit redesign for throughput only

Removing seats, staffing, and dwell-friendly waiting areas treats commute as dead time—not O'Hare's "third place of mobility."

**Why:** Journey time can be social gift, not pure cost (Ch 10).  
**Fix:** [patterns/transit-third-place.md](patterns/transit-third-place.md)

### ❌ Digital layer replacing face-to-face gathering

AR trails, game layers, or social apps that make mediated interaction primary while physical conversation withers.

**Why:** Layering should annotate place, not substitute it; code is law for exclusion (Ch 9).  
**Fix:** [patterns/digital-layers.md](patterns/digital-layers.md)

### ❌ Liveability metrics that ignore children

Adult-oriented coffee-after-2am scores miss child texture-and-variety needs for democratic and physical health.

**Why:** Child-friendly cities require third places where children socialise on their terms (Ch 4).  
**Fix:** [rtp-ch04-child-friendly.md](references/book-summaries/rtp-ch04-child-friendly.md)
